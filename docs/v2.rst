Feature-routed pipeline (Ageas v2)
==================================

:func:`~ageas.run_pipeline` is Ageas's label-free workflow, introduced with
version 0.1. The original workflow (:class:`~ageas.Hangar`,
:func:`~ageas.n_kfold_selection`, :class:`~ageas.Deck`) trains a fixed
panel of models on every feature and keeps the ones that pass a validation
gate. The v2 pipeline differs in three ways:

* it designs a panel for each dataset;
* it narrows every model ("unit") to the features it relies on;
* it integrates only units that help each other.

No step uses progenitor labels; they serve only to score a finished run.

The pipeline
------------

1. **Profile the dataset** (:func:`~ageas.profile_dataset`). From the
   terminal cells, with their labels, and the progenitor cells' features
   alone, it measures:

   * size and input type: sparsity, non-negativity, whole-number counts;
   * how far the progenitors sit from the mature cells:
     nearest-neighbour distance ratio, coverage, neighbourhood AUC;
   * how cells cluster: progenitors in clusters of their own, purity of
     the mature classes' clusters;
   * how ambiguous the progenitors look to a linear model of the mature
     classes;
   * how curved the mature classes' boundary is: linear accuracy, and what
     a nearest-neighbour classifier adds.
2. **Design the panel** (:func:`~ageas.design_panel`). Rules stated before
   any run (below) turn the profile into new unit configurations. Every unit
   (:class:`~ageas.UnitSpec`) records the rules that shaped it and the ones
   that expect it to validate well or poorly. :func:`~ageas.write_panel`
   writes the panel as a :class:`~ageas.Hangar` config folder.
3. **Weight the terminal cells toward the progenitors**
   (:func:`~ageas.shift_weights`). A terminal cell's weight is the odds that
   its nearest neighbours, in the cells' shared principal-component space,
   are progenitors. Weights are scaled to mean 1 and capped at 10, so
   validation favours units that hold up on progenitor-like cells
   (``validation='shift'``). ``validation='ordinary'`` counts every terminal
   cell alike.
4. **Route every unit to its features** (:func:`~ageas.moe_selection`, which
   returns a :class:`~ageas.MoE_Deck`).

   * In each fold, every unit trains on all features.
   * Its own attributions (:func:`~ageas.explain_unit`) rank the features
     (:func:`~ageas.key_features`).
   * A pruning ladder of attribution mass narrows the unit as far as its
     validation loss stays within ``relative_delta`` of the all-feature
     version's, or to the version that validates best
     (:func:`~ageas.search_ladder`).
   * The chosen version predicts the fold's held-out cells. Together the
     folds give every unit an out-of-fold record on all terminal cells.
5. **Follow validation past the rules** (:func:`~ageas.extend_panel`,
   ``refine=True``). Where a family's best-validated unit sits at the edge
   of the range the rules gave it (C, smoothing, tree depth and rounds,
   network size), a second round trains one more unit past that edge, in the
   same folds.
6. **Integrate**, two ways, on the out-of-fold record and under the same
   validation:

   * **The no-harm mixture** (:func:`~ageas.no_harm_selection`) adds units
     by greedy forward selection only while they lower the ensemble's
     log-loss, then drops any it no longer needs.
   * **A committee** of competent units (:func:`~ageas.competent_units`:
     out-of-fold balanced accuracy within ``tolerance`` of the best) is
     thinned to distinct ones (:func:`~ageas.select_committee`). It votes
     (:func:`~ageas.vote`) in log-odds, one voice per model type, and the
     no-harm mixture counts as much as the whole committee.
7. **Check the strategy** (:func:`~ageas.check_strategy`). For each rule:
   do the units it favoured validate better than the ones it disfavoured?
   And how well do the rules' preferences rank the units overall? Reported
   under both validations.
8. **Walk through unlabeled cells** (:func:`~ageas.graph_stage`). It runs
   when the data hold at least as many unlabeled cells as progenitors (for
   example, cells between them and the terminal states, as in a time
   course) and there are two terminal classes. Otherwise, the vote is the
   output.

   * A random walk over a neighbour graph of every cell, stopped at the
     first terminal cell, gives each progenitor its chance of reaching each
     terminal state (:func:`~ageas.graph_fate`). The graph uses Palantir's
     defaults: PCA (or LSI for TF-IDF input), 10 multiscale diffusion
     components, 30 neighbours.
   * :func:`~ageas.fuse` ranks the progenitors by 0.6 × the walk's rank plus
     0.4 × the vote's. It calls the second class for the share the walk
     gives it on average.

Panel rules
-----------

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 22 45 33

   * - Rule
     - Fires when
     - Effect
   * - ``far_progenitors``
     - neighbourhood AUC ≥ 0.9, coverage < 0.5, or half the progenitors in clusters of their own
     - more regularisation, less capacity
   * - ``near_progenitors``
     - AUC < 0.75, coverage ≥ 0.8 and under a fifth in clusters of their own
     - the reverse
   * - ``few_cells`` / ``many_cells``
     - under 1,000 / at least 5,000 terminal cells
     - less / more capacity; network batch size and epochs follow the cell count
   * - ``curved_boundary``
     - a nearest-neighbour classifier adds ≥ 0.03 accuracy, or linear accuracy < 0.9
     - more capacity; flexible variants favoured over linear models
   * - ``straight_boundary``
     - linear accuracy ≥ 0.97 and a flexible model adds < 0.01
     - less capacity; linear models favoured
   * - ``counts``
     - non-negative whole numbers
     - naive Bayes with smoothing 0.1, 1 and 10
   * - ``wide``
     - more than twice as many features as cells
     - L1 logistic regression; trees sample 30% of the features
   * - ``ambiguous_progenitors``
     - the linear model's mean scaled entropy on progenitors ≥ 0.5
     - more regularisation

Every panel holds the following units:

* logistic regressions (three C values);
* a linear SVM (unless cells × features exceeds 10⁸);
* naive Bayes for non-negative input;
* three XGBoost variants: the capacity the rules chose, plus fewer,
  shallower trees and more, deeper ones;
* three MLPs and three ResNets (compact, standard and roomy);
* a compact recurrent network.

Running it
----------

:func:`~ageas.run_pipeline`'s own defaults are simple: for example, the
committee averages probabilities, and the mixture stays out of the vote. The
settings the method was benchmarked with are in the repository's
``revision/v2/protocol.json``; pass its sections:

.. code-block:: python

    import json
    import ageas

    protocol = json.load(open("revision/v2/protocol.json"))
    result = ageas.run_pipeline(
        terminal_corpus,          # labelled terminal cells, e.g. a Multimodal_Corpus
        progenitor_x,             # progenitors' model input, (n_cells, ..., n_features), no labels
        workdir="ageas_v2_run",   # generated panel configs and Lightning logs
        selection=protocol["selection"], routing=protocol["routing"],
        synergy=protocol["synergy"], committee=protocol["committee"],
        validation=protocol["pipeline"]["validation"], refine=protocol["pipeline"]["refine"],
        accelerator="cuda", seed=0,
        graph=all_cells, graph_settings=protocol["graph"],   # optional, see below
    )

``all_cells`` turns on the graph stage. It holds every cell under one
preprocessing:

* ``features``: terminal, query and unlabeled cells;
* ``labels``: terminal class index, ``-1`` for the rest;
* ``query_index``: each progenitor's row, in ``progenitor_x`` order;
* ``kind``: ``"pca"``, or ``"lsi"`` for TF-IDF input.

Without it, the committee's vote is the output.

The :class:`~ageas.PipelineResult` holds:

* ``query["final"]``: the progenitors' fate probabilities. This is the graph
  stage's fusion, or the vote. ``query`` also holds the no-harm mixture
  (``no_harm``), the vote (``vote``) with the committee's ``agreement`` and
  ``disagreement`` per cell, every unit's probabilities (``members``) and the
  walk (``walk``).
* ``profile``, ``panel`` and ``report``: the data profile, the designed
  units and the strategy check.
* ``deck``: the :class:`~ageas.MoE_Deck`. It holds the routed experts, the
  out-of-fold record (``deck.oof``), ``predict`` and ``debrief`` (per-class
  feature importance).
* ``cell_weights``, ``committee`` and ``graph``: the shift-aware weights, the
  committee's members, and the graph stage's record (whether it ran, and
  why).

Protocol
--------

``revision/v2/protocol.json``:

* ``selection``: 5 folds, 10% validation split, stratified;
* ``routing``: ladder at 95%, 80%, 60% and 40% of attribution mass, at
  least 20 features per class; the version that validates best is kept;
* ``synergy``: each unit the mixture takes must lower its log-loss by at
  least 1%;
* ``committee``:

  * competence within 0.05 balanced accuracy;
  * near-duplicate members dropped (mean Jensen-Shannon divergence below
    0.01 bits), as are members siding with the rest on under half the
    cells;
  * a log-odds vote with one voice per model type, the mixture joining it;
  * every routed unit refitted on all terminal cells;
* ``pipeline``: shift-aware validation, second round on;
* ``graph``: at least one unlabeled cell per progenitor, 30 neighbours, 10
  diffusion components, PCA 50, walk weight 0.6.

Its ``_history`` records each change and the evidence behind it. The scripts
that benchmarked the method, compared it with baseline methods, and ablated
its components (reviewer comment R2.4) are in ``revision/v2``.

.. note::

   Keep ``workdir`` outside synced folders such as Dropbox: every fold writes
   Lightning logs and refitted models there.
